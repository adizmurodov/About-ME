Language · Speaking Strategy

Be a good listener

It is important to interact appropriately with the person you are speaking to.

1 · Show interest and empathy

Oh dear…

Oh, I'm sorry…

I didn't realise…

“Oh, I'm sorry. I didn't realise the music was so loud.”

2 · Ask for agreement with question tags

The kitchen is a bit messy, isn't it?

You don't mind, do you?

Positive sentence → negative tag. Negative sentence → positive tag.

2G Speaking · The Diplomatic Flatmate

05

When we talk about a problem, we must listen and react to the other person. First, show that you understand their feelings with phrases like "Oh dear", "Oh, I'm sorry" and "I didn't realise". Second, use question tags to invite the other person to agree with you. They make a statement softer and more friendly. Ask a few students to repeat the example sentences with good intonation.

Language · Question tags

How question tags work

Positive → negative tag

The kitchen is clean, isn't it?

You can cook, can't you?

He paid the rent, didn't he?

Negative → positive tag

You don't smoke, do you?

He hasn't paid, has he?

It isn't fair, is it?

Special cases

I'm late, aren't I?

Let's start, shall we?

Close the door, will you?

Nobody called, did they?

Voice goes down: you expect the other person to agree.

Voice goes up: it's a real question. You aren't sure.

2G Speaking · The Diplomatic Flatmate

06

The tag uses the same auxiliary verb as the sentence: be, do, have, can, will and so on. If the sentence has no auxiliary, we use do, does or did. A positive sentence takes a negative tag, and a negative sentence takes a positive tag. Words like never and nobody make the sentence negative, so the tag is positive. Remember the special cases: I'm becomes "aren't I", Let's becomes "shall we", and a request like "Close the door" becomes "will you". Finally, practise the intonation: when the voice goes down, we expect agreement; when the voice goes up, it is a real question.

Language · Learn this!

be supposed to

We use be supposed to for rules and duties: what other people expect you to do.

The form

am / is / are
+ supposed to
+ verb

Positive

We're supposed to pay the bills on time.

Negative

I'm not supposed to stay out late on weekdays.

Question

Are we supposed to clean the bathroom every week?

Don't forget be! Wrong: We supposed to pay. Right: We're supposed to pay.

2G Speaking · The Diplomatic Flatmate

22

This is the "Learn this!" box from the book. "Be supposed to" shows obligation: something you are expected to do, often because of a rule. The two examples from the book are "We're supposed to pay the bills on time" and "I'm not supposed to stay out late on weekdays". The most common mistake is to forget the verb "be". Ask students for one more example about their own home or school rules.
