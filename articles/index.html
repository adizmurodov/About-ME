01 · The basics

What is an article?

An article is a small word that comes before a noun. It tells the listener: do you mean any one thing, or one specific thing?

Indefinite

a

any one thing, not a specific one

a cat · a book

Indefinite

an

the same as a, but before a vowel sound

an apple · an hour

Definite

the

a specific thing: we both know which one

the cat · the sun

No article

Ø

things in general, and most names

cats · music · Tashkent

English Articles · A, An & The

03

An article is a very small word, but it carries important information. It always comes before a noun, or before an adjective + noun, like "a big house". English has two indefinite articles, a and an, which mean "any one". It has one definite article, the, which means "the specific one we both know". And very often we use no article at all. We write this as Ø, which simply means "nothing here".

01 · The basics

Any one, or the specific one?

A / AN = any one

“Can I borrow a pen?”

Any pen is fine. It doesn’t matter which one.

THE = the specific one

“Can I borrow the pen?”

That exact pen: the one we both know about.

Same noun, different article, different meaning.

English Articles · A, An & The

04

This is the most important idea in the whole lesson. If I say "Can I borrow a pen?", I mean any pen. I don't care which one. If I say "Can I borrow the pen?", I mean one specific pen, for example the pen on your desk that we can both see. The noun "pen" is the same. Only the article changes, and the meaning changes with it.

02 · A or An?

Sound,
not spelling.

Choose a or an by the first sound of the next word, not by its first letter.

Here is the one rule for a and an. We do not look at the first letter of the word. We listen to the first sound. This is why we say "an hour" (the h is silent) but "a university" (it starts with a "you" sound). On the next two slides we will see how this works.

English Articles · A, An & The

06

Use "a" when the next word starts with a consonant sound, like b, c, d or t. Use "an" when the next word starts with a vowel sound: the sounds of a, e, i, o and u. We use "an" because it is easier to say: "an apple" flows better than "a apple". Look at the tip at the bottom: the article only cares about the word right after it. "An apple" becomes "a red apple", because "red" starts with a consonant sound. "A car" becomes "an old car", because "old" starts with a vowel sound.

English Articles · A, An & The

07

These words confuse many learners. "University" starts with the letter u, but we say "you-niversity", which starts with a consonant sound, so we say "a university". "One" starts with a "w" sound, so it is "a one-day trip". On the right, "hour" and "honest" start with a silent h, so the first sound is a vowel: "an hour", "an honest man". Letters in abbreviations are read by their names: M is "em" and X is "eks", so we say "an MBA" and "an X-ray". If you are not sure, say the word out loud and listen.

03 · Using A / AN

When to use a / an

1 First mention

It is new to the listener.

I saw a great film last night.

2 Any one

Which one does not matter.

Can I borrow a pen?

3 Jobs

Say what someone does.

She is an engineer.

4 Describing

Say what kind of thing it is.

It’s a beautiful day.

5 Meaning “one”

A / an = one.

I’ll be back in an hour.

6 “Per” / “every”

How often, how fast, how much.

twice a week · 90 km an hour

English Articles · A, An & The

08

Here are the six most common uses of a / an. One: when we mention something for the first time. The listener doesn't know it yet. Two: when we mean any one, and it doesn't matter which. Three: with jobs. In English we must say "She is an engineer", never "She is engineer". Four: when we describe something or say what kind of thing it is. Five: a / an can simply mean "one": "an hour" is "one hour". Six: to say "per" or "every": "twice a week", "ninety kilometres an hour".

03 · Using A / AN

A / an means one countable thing

Use a / an only with a singular countable noun: something you can count. one book, two books.

With plural or uncountable nouns, use some, or no article.

Common uncountable nouns

water, money, advice, information, news, music, homework, furniture

WrongRight
a booksbooks / some books
a waterwater / a glass of water
an advicesome advice / a piece of advice
an informationsome information
a homeworksome homework
a good newsgood news / some good news

English Articles · A, An & The

09

A / an comes from the old word for "one". So we can only use it with one countable thing. We never say "a books", because "books" is plural. We also never say "a water" or "an advice", because these nouns are uncountable: we cannot count them one by one. Instead we say "some water", "some advice", or we use a container or unit: "a glass of water", "a piece of advice". Be careful: in many languages "advice", "information" and "news" are countable, but in English they are uncountable.

04 · Using THE

When to use the

1 Second mention

Now we know which one.

I saw a film. The film was great.

2 Only one exists

There is only one in the world.

the sun · the moon · the internet

3 We both know

The situation makes it clear.

Please close the door.

4 Made specific

More words tell us which one.

the man who called you

5 Superlatives

best, biggest, most important…

She is the best player.

6 Order and “only”

first, last, next, only, same

the first day · the only way

English Articles · A, An & The

10

"The" always means: the listener knows exactly which one. One: the second time we mention something. First "a film", then "the film". Two: things that are unique, like the sun, the moon or the internet. Three: when the situation makes it clear. If there is one door in the room, it is "the door". Four: when extra words make it specific: "the man who called you", "the book on the table". Five: with superlatives like "the best" or "the biggest", because only one thing can be the best. Six: with first, last, next, only and same, for the same reason.

English Articles · A, An & The

11

Place names are a special area. We use "the" with rivers, seas and oceans, mountain ranges and deserts. We also use it with country names that contain a word like States, Kingdom or Emirates, and with plural names like the Netherlands. But most countries, all cities, continents, single mountains, lakes and streets take no article. So we say "I live in Uzbekistan", not "the Uzbekistan", and "I live in Tashkent", not "the Tashkent". One useful clue: when a name has "of" in it, it usually takes "the": the Republic of Uzbekistan, the Gulf of Mexico.

English Articles · A, An & The

12

Some uses of "the" are fixed. We just need to learn them. We play the piano and play the guitar. "The" + an adjective can mean a whole group of people: the rich, the young, the elderly. We use "the" for a nationality as a whole group: the British, the Chinese. Famous buildings, museums, hotels and many newspapers take "the". We also say "the 1990s" for decades, and "the Smiths" or "the Karimovs" for a whole family.

05 · No article (Ø)

No article: talking in general

When we talk about things in general (all of them, or the idea), we use plural or uncountable nouns with no article.

General → no article (Ø)

Specific → the

Dogs are loyal animals.

The dogs next door are very loud.

I love music.

I love the music in this film.

Water is important for life.

The water in this bottle is cold.

English Articles · A, An & The

13

This is a very common mistake. When we talk about something in general, we use no article. "Dogs are loyal animals" means all dogs. "The dogs next door are very loud" means some specific dogs. "I love music" means music in general. "I love the music in this film" means one specific music. So ask yourself: am I talking about everything in general, or about specific ones? General: no article. Specific: the.

05 · No article (Ø)

No article: common fixed groups

1 Meals

breakfast, lunch, dinner

2 Sports and games

football, tennis, chess

3 Languages, subjects

English, math, history

She speaks English and Uzbek.

4 Days and months

5 Names

people, cities, most countries

6 “by” + transport

bus, car, train, plane

English Articles · A, An & The

14

Here are six groups of words that normally take no article. Meals: have breakfast, have lunch. Sports and games: play football, play chess. Compare this with musical instruments, which take "the": play the piano. Languages and school subjects: speak English, study math. Days, months and years: on Monday, in July, in 2026. Names of people, cities and most countries. And transport after "by": by bus, by car, by train.

05 · No article (Ø)

“Go to school” or “go to the school”?

No article → the main purpose

The → the building or the object

I go to school every day. (I study there.)

Parents came to the school for a meeting.

It’s late. Time to go to bed.

Your phone is on the bed.

He is in hospital with a broken leg.

I went to the hospital to visit him.

Also with no article: at home, at work, at night, go home. In American English, people usually say “in the hospital”.

English Articles · A, An & The

15

Words like school, bed, hospital, prison, church and university work in a special way. When we use the place for its main purpose, we use no article. A student goes to school to study. A tired person goes to bed to sleep. A patient is in hospital. But when we mean just the building or the object, we use "the": parents go to the school for a meeting, your phone is on the bed, you go to the hospital to visit a friend. Also remember these phrases with no article: at home, at work, at night, go home. Note that American English usually says "in the hospital".

English Articles · A, An & The

16

This one chart covers almost every noun. Ask two questions. First: is the noun singular and countable, or is it plural or uncountable? Second: is it specific, meaning we both know which one, or is it general? If it is specific, the answer is always "the", for any kind of noun. If it is general and singular countable, use a or an. If it is general and plural or uncountable, use no article. Finally, if you chose a / an, check the first sound of the next word.

English Articles · A, An & The

18

Let's read a short story together and watch the articles. "A new phone" and "an umbrella" are new information, so we use a / an. After that, the listener knows which phone and which umbrella, so they become "the phone" and "the umbrella". "The first day" takes "the" because of the word "first". "The shop near my house" is made specific by "near my house". "The manager" is the manager of that shop, so we both know who. Finally, "good service" is a general idea, so it has no article.

Here is everything on one page. Take a photo of this slide or write it in your notebook. A / an means any one: one countable thing, the first mention, jobs, "one" and "per", and "an" before a vowel sound. The means the specific one: the second mention, unique things, things we both know, superlatives, and some place names. No article means general: general plurals, general ideas, meals, sports, languages, most countries and cities, and fixed phrases like "go to school" and "by bus".

Remember

Three questions, every time

1

Is it one countable thing, or plural / uncountable?

Plural or uncountable nouns never take a / an.

2

Do we both know which one?

Yes → the. No → a / an, or Ø for things in general.

3

What is the first sound of the next word?

Vowel sound → an. Consonant sound → a.

Thank you! Any questions?

To finish, remember three questions. One: is the noun one countable thing, or is it plural or uncountable? Two: do we both know which one? If yes, use "the". If not, use a / an, or no article for things in general. Three: if you need a / an, what is the first sound of the next word? Practice these three questions every time you speak or write, and articles will soon become automatic. Thank you! Are there any questions?
